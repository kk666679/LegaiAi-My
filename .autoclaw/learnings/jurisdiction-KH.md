# Cambodia — Legal System & Sources

## Identity

- **ISO 3166-1 alpha-2:** `KH`
- **Official name:** Kingdom of Cambodia (Preăh Réachéanachâkr Kâmpŭchéa)
- **Legal tradition:** Civil law (French-derived), with elements of customary
  law and post-1979 socialist legal influences
- **Official language:** Khmer
- **Capital:** Phnom Penh

## Legal system overview

Cambodia's legal system is based on the **civil law tradition** inherited from
the French colonial period. Key features:
1. **Written codes** — the Constitution, Civil Code, Criminal Code, and
   procedural codes are the primary sources
2. **Constitutional Council** — reviews constitutionality of laws
3. **Court structure** — Supreme Court (civil/criminal), Constitutional Council
4. **Mixed influences** — French civil law, UN transitional law (UNTAC period),
   and local customary practices

## Official gazettes

- **Official Gazette of the Kingdom of Cambodia** — publication of laws,
  royal decrees, sub-decrees, and proclamations

## Numbering conventions

Cambodian laws are typically numbered by:
- **Year + sequential number** (e.g., "NS/RKM/0123/24" style notations for
  royal decrees and sub-decrees)
- **NS/RKM/** prefix = "Nation/Religion/King" (standard Khmer law citation)
- **Chapter-based** references for the Codes

## Official languages

- **Khmer** is the sole official language of legislation
- **French** historical influence but not an official legislative language
- **English** translations exist for investment laws and some commercial codes
- **Bilingual support:** Limited official English versions; most authoritative
  texts are in Khmer

## Bilingual / multilingual support

- Investment Law and some commercial statutes have English versions
- Most laws are only available in Khmer
- International organizations sometimes provide English translations

## Retrieval guidance

- Query for `jurisdiction: "KH"`
- Verify source is the Official Gazette or Constitutional Council
- Cross-reference with Globalex guide for portal access details
- Treat Khmer texts as authoritative; English versions as secondary
- Civil code provisions use article numbering

## Legal safety boundary

The system distinguishes **retrieving Cambodian legislation** from **giving
legal advice**. When a provision cannot be verified, the system returns:
> *"Insufficient verified evidence."*

Never fabricate law numbers, article numbers, or legal status.
