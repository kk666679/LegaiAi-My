# Thailand — Legal System & Sources

## Identity

- **ISO 3166-1 alpha-2:** `TH`
- **Official name:** Kingdom of Thailand (Ratcha Anachak Thai)
- **Legal tradition:** Civil law (mixed with customary law and Buddhist legal
  principles), influenced by French, German, and Japanese legal traditions
- **Official language:** Thai
- **Capital:** Bangkok

## Legal system overview

Thailand has a **civil law system** with distinctive features:
1. **Civil and Commercial Code** — foundational private law (modeled on
   European codes)
2. **Constitution** — current 2017 Constitution (previous versions: 2007, 1997)
3. **Court structure** — Constitutional Court, Supreme Court, Supreme
   Administrative Court, Courts of First Instance
4. **Monarchical influence** — lèse-majesté laws (Section 112 of the Penal
   Code) are strictly enforced
5. **Customary law** — traditional practices in family and land matters,
   especially in rural areas

## Official gazettes

- **Royal Thai Government Gazette** — official publication of laws, royal
  decrees, ministerial regulations, and announcements

## Numbering conventions

Thai legislation uses:
- **Public Act (P.A.) / Royal Decree (R.D.) numbering** — sequential within
  each year or session
- **Ministerial Regulations** — issued by individual ministries
- **Organic Acts** — for constitutional implementation
- **Emergency Decrees** — issued by the Cabinet under emergency powers

## Official languages

- **Thai** is the sole official language of legislation
- **English** translations exist for some commercial, investment, and
  tax laws
- **Bilingual support:** Limited official English versions

## Bilingual / multilingual support

- Board of Investment (BOI) laws have English versions
- Some commercial and tax legislation has official English translations
- Unofficial translations are widely available

## Retrieval guidance

- Query for `jurisdiction: "TH"`
- Primary source: RATCHAKIT (ratchakitcha.soc.go.th)
- Verify publication date in the Royal Thai Government Gazette
- Civil and Commercial Code uses book and section numbering
- Treat Thai texts as authoritative; translations as secondary

## Legal safety boundary

The system distinguishes **retrieving Thai legislation** from **giving legal
advice**. When a provision cannot be verified, the system returns:
> *"Insufficient verified evidence."*

Never fabricate law numbers, section numbers, or legal status.
