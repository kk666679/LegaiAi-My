# Philippines — Legal System & Sources

## Identity

- **ISO 3166-1 alpha-2:** `PH`
- **Official name:** Republic of the Philippines
- **Legal tradition:** Mixed — civil law (Spanish-derived) blended with
  American common law (especially in procedure and commercial law), plus
  Islamic law (Moro) and customary law
- **Official languages:** Filipino and English (both official)
- **Capital:** Manila

## Legal system overview

The Philippines has a **unique mixed legal system**:
1. **Civil law** — Spanish colonial influence in private law, family law,
   property, and obligations (Civil Code of 1950)
2. **Common law** — American colonial influence in procedure, evidence,
   corporations, and constitutional law
3. **Islamic law** — Code of Muslim Personal Laws (Presidential Decree 1083)
   for Muslims in Mindanao and Sulu
4. **Constitutional law** — 1987 Constitution, with bill of rights and
   social justice provisions
5. **Court structure** — Supreme Court, Court of Appeals, Regional Trial
   Courts, and specialized courts (e.g., Sandiganbayan, Court of Tax Appeals)

## Official gazettes

- **Official Gazette of the Philippines** — publication of laws, presidential
  issuances, and other government documents

## Numbering conventions

Philippine legislation uses:
- **Republic Act (RA) No. XXXX** — principal statutes enacted by Congress
- **Batas Pambansa (BP) Blg. X** — national laws during Marcos-era Batasang
  Pambansa
- **Presidential Decree (PD) No. XXXX** — decrees from Marcos era
- **Republic Acts** are sequentially numbered since 1946 (currently in the
  10,000+ range)
- **Executive Orders, Memorandum Orders, Administrative Orders** from the
  President

## Official languages

- **Filipino** and **English** are both official languages of legislation
- **Bilingual support:** Most legislation is published in both languages;
  English is often the working language in legal practice
- **Regional languages** (Cebuano, Ilocano, etc.) have no official legislative status

## Bilingual / multilingual support

- Official Gazette provides both Filipino and English versions for recent laws
- LawPhil and ChanRobles provide English texts (some older laws in Spanish
  originals with English translations)

## Retrieval guidance

- Query for `jurisdiction: "PH"`
- Primary retrieval: LawPhil or ChanRobles for full text
- Cross-reference with Official Gazette for official publication date
- Republic Acts are the most common retrieval target
- Verify whether a law has been amended by subsequent legislation

## Legal safety boundary

The system distinguishes **retrieving Philippine legislation** from **giving
legal advice**. When a provision cannot be verified, the system returns:
> *"Insufficient verified evidence."*

Never fabricate Republic Act numbers, section numbers, or legal status.
