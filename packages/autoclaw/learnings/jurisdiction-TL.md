# Timor-Leste — Legal System & Sources

## Identity

- **ISO 3166-1 alpha-2:** `TL`
- **Official name:** Democratic Republic of Timor-Leste (República Democrática
  de Timor-Leste)
- **Legal tradition:** Civil law (Portuguese-derived), with customary law
  (Adat/Tara Bandu) and transitional UN legal influences
- **Official languages:** Tetum and Portuguese (official); Indonesian and
  English (working languages)
- **Capital:** Dili

## Legal system overview

Timor-Leste's legal system reflects its unique history:
1. **Portuguese civil law tradition** — Civil Code, Penal Code, and procedural
   codes based on Portuguese models
2. **UN transitional administration** — UNTAET regulations (1999-2002) formed
   the initial legal framework
3. **Constitution** — 2002 Constitution (the supreme law)
4. **Customary law** — traditional village law (Tara Bandu) recognized for
   community matters
5. **Court structure** — Court of Appeal, District Courts, and specialized
   courts

## ASEAN Status

- **Member state** — Timor-Leste was formally admitted as the
  **11th member state of ASEAN** on **26 October 2025** at the
  47th ASEAN Summit in Kuala Lumpur, Malaysia (Declaration on
  the Admission of Timor-Leste into ASEAN); it acceded to the
  ASEAN Charter on the same occasion
- The system may apply ASEAN regional rules to Timor-Leste
  subject to the standard safeguard: verify whether a specific
  ASEAN agreement has been ratified/implemented domestically
  before treating it as binding national law
- Accession is recent: implementation of ASEAN economic
  agreements may still be in progress — verify instrument-level
  status rather than assuming full alignment
- **Source:** https://asean.org/forging-a-new-era-timor-leste-admitted-into-asean

## Numbering conventions

Timor-Leste uses:
- **Law No. X/YYYY** — Laws passed by the National Parliament
- **Decree-Law No. X/YYYY** — Government legislation
- **Decree No. X/YYYY** — Presidential or Government decrees
- **Ministerial Diploma No. X/YYYY** — Ministerial regulations

## Official languages

- **Tetum** and **Portuguese** are official languages
- **Indonesian** and **English** are working languages
- **Bilingual support:** Many laws are published in Portuguese; Tetum versions
  may exist; English translations are rare and unofficial

## Bilingual / multilingual support

- Some laws have Portuguese-only authoritative texts
- Tetum translations may be available for popular legislation
- English versions are usually unofficial

## Retrieval guidance

- Query for `jurisdiction: "TL"`
- Verify ASEAN membership status from https://asean.org/about-asean/member-states before applying
  any ASEAN-specific rules
- Primary source: Ministry of Justice or Jornal da República
- Treat Portuguese/Tetum texts as authoritative; translations as secondary
- UNTAET regulations remain relevant for historical continuity

## Legal safety boundary

The system distinguishes **retrieving Timor-Leste legislation** from **giving
legal advice**. When a provision cannot be verified, the system returns:
> *"Insufficient verified evidence."*

Never fabricate law numbers, article numbers, or legal status. Verify
current ASEAN membership and instrument-level status from asean.org.
