# ASEAN Institutions & Governance

## Identity

- **Organization:** Association of Southeast Asian Nations (ASEAN)
- **Headquarters:** Jakarta, Indonesia (ASEAN Secretariat)
- **Established:** 8 August 1967 (Bangkok Declaration)
- **Charter in force:** 15 December 2008
- **Working language:** English
- **Current Secretary-General:** [Verify from asean.org]

## Core institutions

### Summit bodies

| Body | Composition | Frequency | Role |
|------|-------------|-----------|------|
| **ASEAN Summit** | Heads of State/Government | Annual (rotating Chair) | Highest policy-making body |
| **ASEAN Coordinating Council (ACC)** | Foreign Ministers | Regular | Coordinate Community Councils |
| **ASEAN Community Council (APSC)** | Ministers (foreign, defence, law, etc.) | Regular | Political-security pillar |
| **ASEAN Economic Ministers (AEM)** | Ministers of economy/trade | Regular | Economic pillar |
| **ASEAN Socio-Cultural Ministers** | Ministers of social sectors | Regular | Socio-cultural pillar |

### Central bodies

| Body | Role |
|------|------|
| **ASEAN Secretariat** | Administrative coordination; research; support to bodies |
| **Committee of Permanent Representatives (CPR)** | Monitor implementation; meet weekly in Jakarta |
| **ASEAN National Secretariats** | Each member state has a national focal point |

### Sectoral bodies

ASEAN has over **30 sectoral ministerial bodies** covering:
- Agriculture and forestry
- Transport
- Tourism
- Information and communications technology
- Energy
- Environment
- Health
- Labour
- Youth
- Culture and the arts
- Science and technology
- And more

### Dispute settlement bodies

| Mechanism | Applicability |
|-----------|---------------|
| **ASEAN Dispute Settlement Mechanism (DSM)** | Economic disputes under ASEAN agreements |
| **ASEAN Protocol on Enhanced Dispute Settlement Mechanism (2004)** | Specific economic agreements |
| **ASEAN Ministerial Meeting diplomatic resolution** | Political/security disputes |
| **Treaty of Amity and Cooperation (TAC) mechanisms** | All TAC parties (includes non-ASEAN states) |

## Specialized entities

| Entity | Mandate |
|--------|---------|
| **ASEAN Intergovernmental Commission on Human Rights (AICHR)** | Promote and protect human rights |
| **ASEAN Commission on the Promotion and Protection of the Rights of Women and Children (ACWC)** | Women and children rights |
| **ASEAN Foundation** | People-to-people cooperation |
| **ASEAN University Network (AUN)** | Higher education cooperation |
| **ASEAN Law Association (ALA)** | Legal profession cooperation |
| **Southeast Asia Regional Centre for Counter-Terrorism (SEARCCT)** | Counter-terrorism capacity building |

## Membership criteria

The ASEAN Charter (Article 4) sets criteria for membership:
1. Located in Southeast Asia
2. Able and willing to carry out obligations of membership
3. Agree to be bound by the Charter
4. Able and willing to participate in all ASEAN activities
5. Agree to accede to all ASEAN treaties, conventions, and agreements

## Decision-making

| Mode | Description |
|------|-------------|
| **ASEAN Way** | Consensus-based, non-confrontational, informal |
| **ASEAN Plus Three** | China, Japan, South Korea (dialogue partners) |
| **ASEAN Regional Forum (ARF)** | Security dialogue (27 participants) |
| **East Asia Summit (EAS)** | Strategic dialogue (18 participants) |

> **Note on consensus:** ASEAN decisions require consensus. A single member
> can block a decision. This is a fundamental structural feature of ASEAN
> governance.

## Legal safety boundary

The system distinguishes **ASEAN institutional decisions** from **national law**:
- ASEAN Summits produce declarations, not national legislation
- ASEAN agreements require domestic incorporation
- Always verify whether an ASEAN decision has been implemented by a specific
  member state before treating it as binding national law

When ASEAN institutional status cannot be verified, the system returns:  
> *"Insufficient verified evidence."*
